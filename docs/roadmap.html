Explainable AI Under Distribution Shift

When Does Heatmap Drift Add Value for Error Detection? An Empirical Study on CIFAR-10-C

An empirical study under distribution shift on CIFAR-10-C. When an image classifier sees blurred, noisy or foggy images, do its explanations change in a way that tells us something about its mistakes that its confidence score doesn't already say?

01 Research question & gap

The project tests whether a finding from tabular machine learning carries over to computer vision, and whether explanations add any monitoring value beyond plain confidence.

📄

Anchor paper

Masood et al. (2026), Explanation audits reveal silent failures of machine learning models under distribution shift, Discover Artificial Intelligence 6, 890. doi:10.1007/s44163-026-02012-6

  • Tabular data, 4 model types, noise / masking / mean shift
  • Per-sample explanation volatility was a weak error detector: AUROC 0.575 vs 0.866 for confidence
  • Batch-level attribution drift tracked error well: Spearman 0.681 vs 0.613 for mean uncertainty
🔀

Two kinds of explanation monitoring

Local volatility: does one image's attribution map change under a small perturbation? Gives a score per image, so it can rank individual predictions.

Batch drift: does the average attribution profile of a whole batch move away from a clean reference? One score per batch. It can raise an alert, but it can't rank images.

Already known

Heatmaps degrade under corruption

Hovakimyan & Bravo (2026) showed Grad-CAM maps drift under noise and blur (CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet). They don't use the drift to detect errors. Degradation alone can't be our claim.

Neighbouring

Consistency beyond confidence

Kalita & Singh (2026) tested output consistency (no heatmaps at all) beyond confidence on CIFAR-10-C. It gave +0.13 AUROC on noise when noise was seen in training, but only +0.002 when noise was unseen.

Open gap

Does heatmap drift add value?

Not found yet: a test of whether IG / Grad-CAM drift adds per-image error-detection value beyond confidence, entropy and corruption severity, on held-out corruption families.

04 Track A vs Track B

These two analyses answer different questions and are kept separate throughout.

Operational drift (frozen): at deployment we never have the clean version of a corrupted image, so the Track A score only uses the input we have:
D(x) = (1/K) · Σₖ d( A(x), A(Tₖ(x)) )
Tₖ = Gaussian pixel noise σ = 0.01 (4× milder than CIFAR-10-C's mildest noise) · K = 8 · d = 1 − Spearman ρ on |IG| maps · target = predicted class. Clean-vs-corrupted drift is a diagnostic only and never a monitoring feature.

05 Hypotheses & experiments

Each hypothesis is written so it can fail. H1 is primary; the rest are secondary.

Primary

H1 · Local heatmap drift adds information beyond confidence and entropy, on unseen corruption families

Test: E4 (confidence + entropy + drift) vs E3 (confidence + entropy), evaluated leave-one-corruption-family-out. Supported only if the pooled ΔAUROC has a cluster-bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero and is positive in at least 3 of the 4 held-out families. Primary setting: ResNet-18, Integrated Gradients, predicted class, 3 seeds.

H2

Batch drift tracks degradation

Batch-level JS drift correlates positively with the error rate of each corruption condition.

H3

Beyond severity

Drift still helps after adding the (oracle) corruption severity (E6 > E5).

H4

Method matters

IG and Grad-CAM give reproducibly different drift utility.

H5

Better selective prediction

Rejecting by the E4 score leaves lower risk than rejecting by the E3 score, at 90 / 80 / 70 / 60% coverage.

H6

More than output instability

Drift adds information beyond output-level consistency, i.e. label stability and confidence deviation (E8 > E7).

One feature at a time: each compared pair differs only by drift (E4 − E3, E6 − E5, E8 − E7). LS = label stability and CD = confidence deviation across the same 8 perturbed copies used for drift, following Kalita & Singh (2026). If E6 does not beat E5, drift is probably just measuring how corrupted the image is. Severity is an oracle variable (unknown at deployment), so E5 and E6 are control analyses, not deployable monitors.

09 Risks & open decisions

⚠️

Perturbation too strong or too weak

If σ is too large, drift just measures sensitivity to an extra corruption. σ = 0.01, K = 8 is frozen; σ ∈ {0.005, 0.01, 0.02} and K ∈ {4, 8, 16} are reported as a sensitivity analysis.

📚

Close prior work

Hovakimyan & Bravo and Kalita & Singh (2026) are close. Position the paper as the explanation-level counterpart of Kalita & Singh's output-level test, and claim only incremental value, not degradation.

🔁

Transfer across corruption families

A comparable output signal failed on unseen families in Kalita & Singh (+0.002 AUROC, not significant). Leave-one-family-out evaluation is the main test.

🎯

IG baseline choice

Integrated Gradients depends on the baseline image (Kindermans et al.). Fix a black baseline and record it in the protocol.

🔍

Grad-CAM is coarse

Only 4×4 on a CIFAR ResNet-18, so it may look stable partly because it is low-resolution. Report the resolution with every result.

⏱️

Scope creep

12 weeks is ambitious. ViT-Tiny and CIFAR-100-C are extensions; a complete ResNet-18 study comes first.

Integrity commitment: negative results will be reported. No claim of incremental value without held-out results and uncertainty intervals.
